# Map prior art, surveyed 2026-11-02

A survey made for the Hub's board (docs/hub-plan.md D124, D134, D135):
what already drives an RPG world map on the web, read from each project's
readme and source on github.com on 2026-11-02 and 2026-11-03. The
figures are what the pages showed that day. Nothing here is DES code; the
decisions it fed are in the plan. The survey was made in two passes: five
angles over projects (SillyTavern extensions, AI roleplay apps, JS
libraries, RPG map tools, worldbuilding tools), then three angles over the
engines themselves (layered zoom, terrain, pan-zoom-pins).

## What it settled

- Nobody has shipped the hub as such: a picture with nodes where a click
  starts a fresh AI chat with the people present.
- The map half is well trodden. Leaflet with `CRS.Simple` is what nearly
  every open-source game map and worldbuilding map runs on; the rest
  hand-roll a CSS-transform camera. The Hub takes Leaflet 1.9.4,
  vendored (D134).
- The closest existing thing is tjalis/Mundi, a SillyTavern extension
  with a picture map, pins at percent coordinates, people on the pins,
  a wheel, drag and pinch camera, nested maps and a click that opens a
  scene; AGPL-3.0 like DES, TypeScript, and its scenes are its own screen
  rather than a chat. Its interaction rules are worth copying.
- Nodes are kept as fractions of the picture (D135): the convention of
  Mundi, General_Map and the Obsidian map plugins, projected to Leaflet's
  pixel units at render time.
- For the later WoW-like map: a tile pyramid cut from one big picture
  (gdal2tiles and leaflet-rastercoords, or Zoomify, or `vips dzsave`),
  swapped base layers for continent, zone and subzone, and a terrain
  renderer such as Red Blob's mapgen4 or Azgaar's generator to bake the
  pictures.
